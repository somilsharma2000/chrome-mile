# Chrome Yatra — Pitch Kit

Public on purpose: how this campaign is positioned and pitched. Built from
documented precedents (the Million Dollar Homepage, Buy My Face,
IWearYourShirt, privateer livery sponsorships) — what made them work was
scarcity + press value + proof, never begging.

## Positioning
**Chrome Yatra** — the livery that rides India.
One chrome Royal Enfield Continental GT 650 (Mr. Clean). Twelve decal lots,
one brand each. The machine ships in full livery, then tours India
GPS-logged end to end. Sponsors get the placement, the proof and the
imagery — the press hook is real, not promised.

## One-liners (pick per context)
- "Twelve brands will ride across India on one chrome GT 650. One of them is yours."
- "A rolling livery, GPS-proven, with your name on it."
- "Hoardings stand still. Your mark rides."
- "One brand per lot. Twelve lots. Then the inventory is gone."

## The ask (WhatsApp cold outreach, ~80 words)
> Hi [Name] — Chrome Yatra is a curated livery auction: 12 exclusive decal lots on a chrome Royal Enfield GT 650 that tours India after the livery completes. Your logo rides every documented kilometre — GPS-verified ride certificates, professional install photos, and usage rights for your own marketing. One brand per lot. Bidding closes 26 Jan 2027, and the campaign ships complete or refunds everyone. Lot [X] opens at ₹[price]. Want the sponsor pack with your logo mocked on the bike?

## Follow-up (48 hours, one message only)
> Sharing the sponsor pack anyway — the lot map and the auction rules are public, and [lot] is still open. The full-livery-or-refund rule means there's no downside to bidding early: you set the floor, everyone else climbs. Closing is Republic Day 2027.

## Objection handling
- **"Is this a scam? I've seen fake car-wrap offers."** — "Fair. That's why everything is in writing before you pay: public auction rules, a signed agreement, 30/70 payments where 70% is due only after install photos, and a full-refund void clause. Payment gateway receipts, never personal transfers."
- **"What impressions do I get?"** — "Not the wrap-industry kind. You get a documented journey: certificates, a public ride archive, and imagery for your own marketing. The value is the story and the proof, not a CPM."
- **"Too expensive."** — "It opens at ₹[price] — a one-time placement, not a monthly retainer, with deliverables in writing. Compare one mid-tier influencer reel: ₹10,000–35,000 for a post that vanishes."
- **"What if you never ride?"** — "Then it's visible: every tour is GPS-logged and published, and the agreement refunds the balance if install photography slips past 60 days. Evidence, not promises."

## Target brand types (in order of fit)
1. Riding gear and accessories (Rynox-class, helmets, apparel)
2. Lubricants, tyres, fuel additives
3. Afterparts and custom houses
4. D2C brands chasing a road-trip association (beverages, apparel, apps)
5. Touring-route businesses: cafes, stays, service networks

## Rules for the operator
- Never ask for support, donations or "help". Sell the lot.
- Price is stated first, story second, sympathy never.
- Every claim made in a pitch must exist in the public documents.
